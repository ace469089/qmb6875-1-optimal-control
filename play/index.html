How the bot thinks

Let V be the value of waiting. If you wait, next period you will take the better of the new offer and waiting again, so

V = 0.9 × E[max(w, V)] = 0.9 × (1 + V²)/2 → V* = (1 − √(1 − 0.81)) / 0.9 = 0.627

So the bot accepts any offer of at least 0.627 and waits otherwise. On average no rule can do better. You can still beat it on a lucky streak; over many rounds you can't.

How the bot thinks
V(s) = max{ s − K , −h + β·E[V(s′) | s] }

Selling gets better one-for-one as the premium rises; holding depends on today's premium only through next week's. The two lines cross once, so the best policy is a single cutoff. That is the professor's control-limit policy C(r, F, K). This page computes it live with Tauchen's method and value iteration, the same way the Python repo does.
